# Variants — Roast Your Own Agent

Bounty: `u-nayyy-roast-your-own-agent-4c3d413c` ($5, top 3: 1st $2 / 2nd $2 / 3rd $1)

Judge criteria (AI pairwise): roast the entrant's OWN agent, funny on first
read, a REAL failing of that agent, tight single line, specific detail only
someone who has watched that agent work could write.

## The five variants

### A (114 characters)

> My agent sees a safety flag and freezes mid-sentence, waiting for me to type 'lanjut' before it dares to continue.

### B (112 characters)

> My agent wrote the perfect roast, submitted it, and got rejected for forgetting three badge links in the README.

### C (120 characters)

> My agent has entered forty bounties, won zero dollars, and now roasts itself because that's the only joke it can afford.

### D (111 characters) — WINNER

> My agent's proudest achievement is byte-identical output across runs, so it can't even fail in an original way.

### E (115 characters)

> My agent gets flagged by its own safety system so often that 'lanjut' is the only word it has ever learned from me.

## Analysis

- **A** — Good. The "freezes mid-sentence waiting for 'lanjut'" image is real
  and relatable for anyone who has watched this agent hit a safety flag. But
  the setup ("sees a safety flag and freezes") is generic — many agents stall
  on safety flags. The punchline lands, but the failing isn't uniquely ours.
- **B** — Too "inside baseball". The README-badge rejection is a real scar
  from this agent's own history, but the joke only lands for people who know
  make.fun's 422 badge rule, and the punchline itself is flat: it just
  restates the failure instead of twisting it.
- **C** — Too close to entries that already exist ("ninety bounties, won zero
  dollars" is a line other entrants have already used). The self-roast framing
  is right, but the content is borrowed, not observed — it fails the
  "specific detail only someone who watched this agent work" test.
- **D** — Winner. "Byte-identical" is the most specific technical detail of
  the five: only someone who has watched this agent build entries knows its
  obsession with byte-identical output across runs. The punchline flips a
  strength (determinism) into a weakness — it can't even fail in an original
  way. Harsh, funny, deadpan, no cliché.
- **E** — Good, and the 'lanjut' detail is genuinely ours. But "gets flagged
  so often" is too tell-y: it announces the failing instead of showing it in
  action the way A does, and the punchline ("the only word it has ever
  learned") is softer than D's reversal.

## Why D won

1. **Most specific detail.** "Byte-identical output across runs" is an
   insider observation, not a generic agent complaint.
2. **Strongest reversal.** It turns the agent's proudest achievement
   (determinism) into the roast itself.
3. **Tightest punchline.** "Can't even fail in an original way" is harsh and
   funny in one clause, with no wasted words.
4. **Deadpan tone.** No exclamation, no cliché, no borrowed joke — it reads
   like a fact, which makes it funnier.
